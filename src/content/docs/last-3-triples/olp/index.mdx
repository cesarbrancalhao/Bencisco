**OLP (One-Look Pair Formation)** completes pair formation in a single look, setting up [TCP](../tcp/). In these cases the triangles have symmetry about the vertical. The diagrams mask everything but the recognition stickers.

Two solutions are given per case, for even and odd permutation states respectively. For full TCP, choose the shortest. A `*` marks the last flip and indicates where 2-flip avoidance can be used (simply swap between H and S).

Specific notation: H = right hedge `(R B' R' B)`, S = right sledge `(R' L R L')`, S' = left sledge `(L R' L' R)`, and `(Uv)` is a whole-puzzle rotation in the U direction. All algs are CIF.

### Case notes

- **Case 1** — You can cancel into a TCP for even permutations very easily here. For odd TCP solutions, be sure to flip the two unsolved triangles to force the 9-mover.
- **Case 2** — This is a case that can be forced into a TCP skip every time, which is recommended.
- **Case 3** — Another case that can sometimes be forced into a TCP skip, but still important to know.
- **Case 4a** — This is the pure 1-flip case for even permutation states. Full TCP is recommended.
- **Case 4b** — The mirror image of the previous 4a case.
- **Case 4c** — Both solutions are equal in length, so for full TCP choose the one that suits you the best.
- **Case 5** — Easy to recognize and both solutions are great.
- **Case 6a** — This is one flip away from PF for odd permutation states. Full TCP will speed this one up.
- **Case 6b** — The mirror image of the previous 6a case.
- **Case 6c** — The dreaded odd-permutation PF for basic TCP users. This is a hedge into case 3.
- **Case 7** — This is three flips away from a completed PF. However you can go straight into the solutions without rotating which is nice.
- **Case 8** — Both the even/odd cases can be forced into a TCP skip, but the even permutation is the only case really worth learning.
